A spouse who walks into a first consultation about a high-asset California divorce is usually doing math in their head. They've heard a number from a friend, a number from a financial advisor, and a wildly different number from the internet. None of those numbers were calibrated to the actual structure of their case. This page is the honest range — what attorney fees, expert costs, and timeline look like across the spectrum of high-asset divorces in California, with notes on what moves the dollar number up or down.

## The honest range

For California high-asset divorces — typically defined as cases involving more than $1 million in combined marital and separate property — total legal and expert costs typically fall between **$25,000 and $250,000** per spouse. The wide range exists because two cases with similar net-worth labels can have radically different complexity. Where the case lands inside that range depends on five factors: whether either spouse owns or controls a closely-held business, whether deferred compensation (stock options, RSUs, carried interest) is in play, whether retirement accounts will require QDRO drafting, whether tracing is needed to support separate-property claims, and — most importantly — whether the case settles or goes to trial.

## What attorneys actually bill

California family law attorneys handling high-asset matters generally bill **$400 to $800 per hour**. In San Diego, $500 to $650 is the most common range for an attorney with 10+ years of family-law experience. Senior partners at boutique firms or specialists with significant trial records bill at the higher end; mid-level associates at the same firms bill $325 to $450.

Most firms require a refundable retainer at engagement. Typical retainer ranges for high-asset matters:

- **Pre-filing strategy + financial disclosure prep:** $7,500 - $15,000
- **Standard contested case (no business valuation, no trial):** $20,000 - $50,000
- **Contested case with business valuation or significant separate-property tracing:** $50,000 - $150,000
- **Trial-track case with multiple experts:** $150,000+

The retainer is replenished as it depletes. If the case resolves more cheaply, the unused portion is refunded.

## Expert costs are often the biggest line item

In high-asset divorces, the experts — not the attorneys — often consume the majority of the budget. The common ones:

**Forensic accountant.** $300 - $500 per hour. Used for tracing separate property, analyzing business income for support calculations, and identifying undisclosed income. Total engagements typically run $5,000 - $50,000.

**Business valuator.** Flat fees of $15,000 - $75,000 for a standalone business valuation, depending on business size and complexity. If the valuation is challenged at trial, expect another $10,000 - $25,000 in deposition and trial-prep time.

**Vocational evaluator.** $5,000 - $15,000. Used when one spouse claims they can't earn at their pre-marriage level — the evaluator analyzes their actual earning capacity for spousal-support purposes under Cal. Fam. Code § 4320.

**QDRO specialist.** $1,500 - $5,000 per plan. Required to divide most retirement accounts post-judgment. Often missed in budget planning.

**Real-estate appraiser.** $500 - $1,500 per property. Routine but required for any disputed real-property valuation.

## What pushes costs up

A few specific case characteristics move total cost dramatically:

**A closely-held business.** Business valuation + the disputes that follow it are the single largest cost driver. Expect $30,000 - $100,000+ in expert fees alone, plus attorney time arguing over methodology.

**Stock options, RSUs, or carried interest.** Each requires a separate valuation methodology. The vesting schedule, time-rule allocation under *In re Marriage of Hug* and *In re Marriage of Nelson*, and tax characterization all become litigation issues.

**Multiple real properties.** Each property is a separate appraisal + potential capital-gains analysis + potential refinance or buyout calculation.

**Tracing separate property.** When commingled funds need to be traced back to a pre-marriage source — a gift, an inheritance, or a pre-marriage account — forensic accounting hours add up quickly. A clean paper trail keeps this cheap; a commingled 20-year marriage with rolled-over accounts can run $20,000+ in tracing alone.

**Trial.** A two- to four-day family-law trial typically adds $40,000 - $120,000 to the total. Most California family-law cases settle before trial; the cases that don't are usually the ones where the cost calculus failed early.

## What keeps costs down

Cases that resolve via mediation or four-way settlement conferences are dramatically less expensive than litigated cases — typically by a factor of three to five. Couples who can stay out of motion practice (no discovery motions, no contempt motions, no emergency requests) keep total spend in the lower half of the range.

Clean financial records help. So does early disclosure under California's mandatory disclosure rules — the spouse who turns over the full financial picture at the first preliminary declaration of disclosure (FL-140 + FL-150) tends to spend less than the spouse who treats discovery as a chess game.

## Timeline

California requires a minimum six-month waiting period from the date of service before a divorce can be finalized (Cal. Fam. Code § 2339). For high-asset cases, the realistic timeline is **12 to 24 months** from petition to judgment. Cases involving business valuation or active litigation often run 18 to 30 months. The cases that finish at the six-month mark are uncontested cases with complete agreement on every issue at filing — uncommon at this asset level.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is the cost range so wide?

The honest answer is that two high-asset cases with similar net worth can have wildly different complexity. A $5 million estate that's mostly publicly-traded equities and one house is a different case than a $5 million estate that includes a privately-held business, a vacation home with tenants, and stock options vesting over four years. The dollar number is a poor proxy for case complexity; the structure of the assets is what drives cost.

### Is there a way to get a better estimate before filing?

A consultation with an attorney who has handled cases at this asset level can produce a realistic budget within a $30,000 - $50,000 range for most clients. The estimate gets tighter once initial disclosures are exchanged and the actual scope of the dispute becomes clear.

### What's the cheapest way to get through a high-asset divorce?

Settlement, with both sides represented by experienced family-law counsel, with the support of a single neutral forensic accountant and a single neutral business valuator. This collaborative or mediation-adjacent approach can resolve cases for under $50,000 per spouse even at the $5M-$10M asset level — when both spouses are willing to engage. When one or both spouses approach the case as a fight to win, the cost calculus changes immediately.

### Does the higher-earning spouse pay the other spouse's attorney fees?

In appropriate cases, yes. California Family Code § 2030 allows the court to order one spouse to contribute to the other's attorney fees when there is a need on one side and an ability to pay on the other. § 2030 motions are routine in high-asset cases where one spouse controls the marital cash flow.

### Should I expect a retainer to be enough for the whole case?

Usually not. Retainers are starting deposits, not flat fees. As the retainer depletes, the firm requests replenishment. For high-asset matters, expect to replenish two to four times over the course of an 18-month case.

### Can the cost ever exceed the estimates above?

Yes. A small percentage of cases involve litigation over hidden assets, allegations of breach of fiduciary duty under Cal. Fam. Code § 1101, or multi-jurisdictional issues that push costs above $250,000. These cases are uncommon but not rare.
