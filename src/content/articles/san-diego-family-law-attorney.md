A parent trying to understand where their custody case will be heard, a spouse weighing whether to file for divorce, a service member at Naval Base San Diego wondering how military benefits factor into a separation — these are the situations that bring people to this page. San Diego County's family law system is governed by the California Family Code, but the local court structure, courthouse locations, and the county's large military population shape how cases actually unfold here.

## Where San Diego County family law cases are heard

San Diego Superior Court — Family Law Division operates from four primary courthouses, with geographic routing based on the petitioner's residence:

**Downtown San Diego — Hall of Justice.** 1100 Union Street, San Diego, CA 92101. The primary courthouse for cases originating in central San Diego, La Jolla, Pacific Beach, Mission Bay, Hillcrest, North Park, and Point Loma. Most contested family law trials in San Diego County are heard here.

**Vista Courthouse — North County.** 325 S. Melrose Drive, Vista, CA 92081. Handles cases for residents of Oceanside, Carlsbad, Encinitas, San Marcos, Escondido, Vista, and surrounding North County communities. Most North County family law cases never need to travel south.

**El Cajon Courthouse — East County.** 250 E. Main Street, El Cajon, CA 92020. Handles cases for residents of El Cajon, La Mesa, Santee, Lemon Grove, Spring Valley, Lakeside, and Alpine. Family law calendar shares the courthouse with other civil matters.

**Chula Vista Courthouse — South Bay.** 500 Third Avenue, Chula Vista, CA 91910. Handles cases for residents of Chula Vista, Imperial Beach, National City, San Ysidro, and the South Bay generally.

For self-represented parties, the [San Diego Superior Court — Family Law Division](https://www.sdcourt.ca.gov) website provides courthouse-specific filing instructions, fee schedules, and forms.

## The military factor

San Diego is one of the most military-concentrated metro areas in the United States. The presence of Naval Base San Diego, Camp Pendleton, MCAS Miramar, Naval Amphibious Base Coronado, and several Coast Guard stations means that a meaningful share of family law cases in this county involve active-duty service members or veterans.

That changes the legal landscape. Military divorces add the federal Uniformed Services Former Spouses' Protection Act (10 U.S.C. § 1408) on top of California community property law. The 10/10 rule, the Survivor Benefit Plan election window, and the Mansell limitation on disability-converted retired pay are recurring issues. The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA) provides procedural protections for active-duty service members in civil proceedings.

A San Diego family law attorney who handles military cases regularly is generally better-positioned than one who handles them occasionally. The federal layer is its own body of law.

## What a first consultation looks like

A first consultation with a San Diego family law attorney is typically 30 to 60 minutes. The attorney listens to the situation, asks the questions that matter most for the specific facts (residency, marriage length, children, income, assets), and gives a realistic read on what the process ahead looks like — what California law says about the situation, where the likely points of dispute are, and what a reasonable budget and timeline look like.

There is no obligation to retain the firm after the consultation. Many firms — including San Diego Family Law Advocates — offer the first consultation free.

## What to bring (or have ready)

The consultation is more productive when the attorney has a concrete picture of the financial and family situation. Documents that help:

- Tax returns from the last two years
- Recent pay stubs for both spouses
- Bank account statements (recent balances and recent activity)
- Mortgage statements, vehicle loan documents, retirement account statements
- A description of the current custody or parenting arrangement, if children are involved
- The marriage certificate, if readily available
- Any existing court orders or agreements

Nothing needs to be in a binder. An attorney can work with what is available.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a San Diego attorney specifically, or can I work with an attorney from elsewhere in California?

A California-licensed attorney can represent you anywhere in California. Practically, a San Diego attorney generally has the advantage of knowing the local judges, the local FCS mediators, and the practical patterns of the San Diego courthouses. For cases that are likely to go to trial or involve multiple court appearances, local counsel is meaningful.

### How do I check whether an attorney is licensed and in good standing?

The State Bar of California maintains a public attorney search at [apps.calbar.ca.gov/attorney/licenseSearch/quick](https://apps.calbar.ca.gov/attorney/licenseSearch/quick). Anyone can verify that an attorney is currently licensed and in good standing in about thirty seconds.

### What's the average cost of a divorce in San Diego?

Range-based: $5,000 to $25,000 for an uncontested or low-conflict divorce; $25,000 to $250,000+ per spouse for a high-asset or high-conflict case. The variation comes from case complexity, not from San Diego pricing being different from the rest of California.
