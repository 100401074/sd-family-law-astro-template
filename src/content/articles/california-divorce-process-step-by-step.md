Someone who has decided to file for divorce in San Diego — or is close to that decision — is often looking for a clear map of what comes next. The process has a defined sequence, but it is not always obvious from the outside which steps are mandatory, which are optional, and where the real delays tend to occur. This guide walks through each stage of a California divorce from the initial residency check through the entry of a final judgment, with specific references to San Diego Superior Court resources and the Judicial Council forms involved at each stage.

## Before you start

A California divorce is a court proceeding. It is governed by the California Family Code (Family Code §§ 2310 et seq.) and the California Rules of Court. In San Diego County, all family law matters are heard at the San Diego Superior Court — Family Law Division. The downtown courthouse handles cases for residents of central San Diego; the Vista and El Cajon courthouses handle cases for North County and East County residents respectively.

Self-represented parties can use the California Courts self-help center at [selfhelp.courts.ca.gov](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov), which provides Judicial Council forms and step-by-step procedural guidance. The forms are free; the filing fees are not. Standard filing fees in San Diego County for a Petition for Dissolution are listed at [sdcourt.ca.gov](https://www.sdcourt.ca.gov); fee waivers are available under FW-001 for qualifying parties.

## When the process gets more complicated

The six-step structure above describes the standard path. Several scenarios add complexity:

**Contested custody.** When parents cannot agree on custody or visitation, the case is routed to Family Court Services for mediation. In San Diego County, FCS uses a recommending model — if mediation fails, the mediator submits a written recommendation to the judge. Custody evaluations under California Rule of Court 5.220 may be ordered in higher-conflict cases.

**Hidden income or unreported assets.** Forensic accounting may be required to trace separate property, identify undisclosed income, or value a closely-held business. This adds significant cost and time.

**Domestic violence.** Cases involving allegations of domestic violence require Domestic Violence Restraining Order (DVRO) proceedings under the Domestic Violence Prevention Act (Cal. Fam. Code §§ 6200 et seq.). The § 3044 presumption shifts the custody analysis significantly.

**Bifurcation.** Under Cal. Fam. Code § 2337, the court can bifurcate the marital status from the financial issues — meaning the marriage can be terminated before the property and support issues are resolved. This is sometimes used in high-asset cases or where one party needs to remarry quickly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a California divorce take?

The minimum is six months and one day from the date of service under Cal. Fam. Code § 2339. Uncontested cases where the parties have reached complete agreement can finalize close to that minimum. Contested cases typically take 12 to 24 months; cases involving business valuations or trial can run longer.

### Can we both use the same attorney?

No. California Rules of Professional Conduct prohibit an attorney from representing both spouses in a divorce due to the inherent conflict of interest. One spouse can hire an attorney and the other can be self-represented, but a single attorney cannot represent both parties. Mediation is a separate path — a neutral mediator works with both spouses but does not represent either.

### What if I can't afford the filing fee?

The fee can be waived under Judicial Council Form FW-001 for qualifying parties. Eligibility is based on income relative to federal poverty guidelines or receipt of certain public benefits. The form is filed with the petition and reviewed by the court clerk.
